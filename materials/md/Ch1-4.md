## Slide 13 — Exercise 1 | Power Consumption

Exercise 1 | Power Consumption

A processor is redesigned with 80% of the original capacitive load, 90% of the original voltage, and 120% of the original clock frequency.

Calculate P_new / P_old. Is the new dynamic power higher or lower?

Answer

P_new / P_old = 0.8 × (0.9)² × 1.2= 0.7776

P_new ≈ 77.8% of P_old

Dynamic power decreases by ≈ 22.2%.

Key Point: Power is proportional to C and f, but to V² ; voltage scaling has a particularly strong effect on power.


## Slide 14 — Exercise 2 | Voltage and Clock Frequency

Exercise 2 | Voltage and Clock Frequency

A processor increases its clock frequency by 44% while its capacitive load remains unchanged.

By what percentage must the voltage be reduced to keep the same dynamic power?

Answer

1.44 × (V_new / V_old)² = 1

V_new / V_old = √(1 / 1.44) = 0.8333

Voltage reduction = 1 − 0.8333 ≈ 16.7%

Key Point: When clock rate increases, voltage must decrease to maintain the same power, illustrating a key limitation behind the Power Wall.


## Slide 15 — Exercise 3 | SPEC Benchmark

Exercise 3 | SPEC Benchmark

A reference machine takes 40 s, 90 s, and 160 s for three benchmarks.
A new processor takes 20 s, 30 s, and 40 s.

Calculate the three performance ratios and their geometric mean.

Answer

R₁ = 40 / 20 = 2        R₂ = 90 / 30 = 3        R₃ = 160 / 40 = 4

Geometric Mean = ∛(2 × 3 × 4) = ∛24 ≈ 2.88

Key Point: SPEC normalizes each benchmark to a reference machine and uses the geometric mean to summarize overall performance.


## Slide 16 — Exercise 4 | MIPS Pitfall

Exercise 4 | MIPS Pitfall

Processor A executes 600M instructions in 0.4 s.
Processor B executes 1000M instructions in 0.5 s for the same task.

Calculate the MIPS of each processor. Which processor has better performance?

Answer

MIPS_A = 600 / 0.4 = 1500 MIPS

MIPS_B = 1000 / 0.5 = 2000 MIPS

But:  T_A = 0.4 s < T_B = 0.5 s

Processor A has better performance.

Key Point: Higher MIPS does not necessarily mean higher performance; execution time should be used for the same workload.


## Slide 17 — Exercise 5 | Amdahl’s Law and Multicore

Exercise 5 | Amdahl’s Law and Multicore

80% of a program can be parallelized. On four cores, assume the parallel portion achieves an ideal 4× speedup; the remaining 20% is unchanged.
(a) Calculate the overall speedup.
(b) What is the maximum speedup as the number of cores approaches infinity?

Answer

(a) New time = 0.20 + 0.80 / 4 = 0.40

Overall speedup = 1 / 0.40 = 2.5×

(b) As cores → ∞:  New time → 0.20

Maximum speedup = 1 / 0.20 = 5×

Key Point: Multicore speeds up only the parallelizable portion; the serial portion limits the maximum overall speedup.
